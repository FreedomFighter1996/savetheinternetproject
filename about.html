About

Small, stubborn, and written down.

This page exists so you can work out whether to trust the rest of the site. That means saying who is doing this and what the rules are, before saying anything impressive.

Origin

Why it started.

It started with a broken link. Then a folder of them. Work that mattered, hosted somewhere that no longer exists, gone without an announcement and without an archive.

That is a small thing to be annoyed about and a large thing to think about. If the record can disappear that easily, then most of what people are making right now is temporary in a way they have not agreed to and mostly do not realise.

This project is the attempt to do something about it that is more useful than complaining and more honest than pretending to have a grand plan. It is one person starting, in public, hoping to not be one person for long.

Operating rules

Six rules this runs on.

Written down so they can be held against us. If the project breaks one of these, that is a legitimate reason to stop paying attention to it.

  1. Nothing is deleted

    Published entries stay up, mistakes included. Corrections go underneath with a date, and the original stays visible. A project about disappearing records cannot quietly edit its own.

  2. No tracking, ever

    No analytics, no pixels, no cookies, no accounts. We do not know who you are or how many of you there are, and that is the correct trade for a site making this argument.

  3. Sources or silence

    Every number carries a link. If a claim cannot be sourced, it gets stated as opinion or it does not get stated. Persuasive and unverified is the failure mode to avoid.

  4. Ship small, ship finished

    A rough thing that works today beats a good thing described in a roadmap. Scope creep is how volunteer projects die, and this one intends to be boring about it.

  5. Disagreement in public

    If someone makes a strong counterargument, it gets published alongside the response, not buried. The argument gets better by being attacked, or it was not much of one.

  6. No money, no owner

    No funding, no sponsors, no acquisition. The entire value of this is that there is nothing to buy, so nobody needs to wonder who is paying for the opinion.

Questions

The obvious ones.

Who is actually behind this?

Currently one person, working on it around other commitments. The name and a way to reach them belongs here, and will be filled in before this goes public.

Is this affiliated with any other campaign or organisation?

No. There is no parent organisation, no political party, no company and no funding behind it. If that ever changes it will be disclosed on this page and in the log on the day it happens.

Is this anti-AI?

No. The objection is to incentives that reward volume over care, which predates the current wave of tools by about twenty years. Tools are not the argument. What they get pointed at is.

Why should anyone listen to a project with no track record?

They should not, yet. That is the honest answer. The daily log exists so the track record accumulates in public where it can be checked, rather than being asserted. Judge it in a month.

What happens if this stops?

The site and the log stay up, with a final entry saying it stopped and why. Everything is plain files in a public repository, so anyone who wants to continue it can, without asking permission.

How do I get in touch?

Through the form on the contribute page, or by email once that address is published here.

That is the whole disclosure.

If it holds up, the useful next step is on the contribute page. If it does not, the counterargument is genuinely welcome and will be published.
