The pitch

Why this is worth your time.

The short version, then the long one. If you only read the box below, you have the argument.

In one paragraph

The open web still exists, but almost nobody arrives there on purpose any more. Discovery, hosting, identity and payment all run through a small number of companies whose incentives point away from yours. That arrangement is not a law of physics. It is a set of defaults, and defaults can be changed by enough people choosing differently and making it easy for the next person to do the same. This project exists to make that choice cheap, obvious and well documented.

What actually happened

Nobody stole it. We rented it out.

The story is usually told as a heist. It was closer to a slow lease.

Every step made sense on its own. Hosting your own site was hard, so people moved to platforms that did it for free. Being found was hard, so people optimised for whatever the search engine rewarded. Getting paid was hard, so people accepted advertising, and advertising demanded measurement, and measurement demanded surveillance.

At each step somebody traded a small amount of control for a large amount of convenience. That is a rational trade. Repeated across two decades and four billion people, it produced a web where the exits are technically open and practically unused.

The consequence shows up as a feeling before it shows up as a fact. Things you wrote are gone. Links you saved go nowhere. The page you land on was not chosen by you, is not written for you, and increasingly was not written by a person at all.

The ask

Five things we are actually pushing for.

Concrete enough to argue with. If you think one of these is wrong, that is a useful conversation and we would rather have it in public.

  1. A domain of your own should be the normal starting point

    Not a profile on someone else's system. The first thing a person makes online should be something they hold the keys to, and moving it should never mean losing it.

  2. Exporting your work should be a right, not a feature

    In an open format, complete, without waiting three days for a zip file that arrives broken. If a service cannot be left easily, it is not a service. It is a hostage situation with a nice interface.

  3. Reading should not be logged

    The default assumption of the web should be that looking at a page leaves no permanent record of who looked. Everything else is a negotiation, and right now only one side is at the table.

  4. Links should be treated as public infrastructure

    Archiving, redirects and permanence are not nice extras. A civilisation that cannot cite its own recent past has a real problem, and it is getting worse quickly.

  5. Human work should be findable

    Not privileged, not subsidised, just findable. If the only viable discovery path rewards volume, then volume is what the web will be made of.

Honest limits

What this is not.

Stating the boundaries early is how a campaign avoids turning into a mood. Here is what we will not be doing, and why.

Not this

Not a return to the old web

The early web was open and also small, hostile to newcomers, and available mostly to people who already knew how it worked. Accessibility and ease are not the enemy.

Not this

Not a new platform

Building one more place to post would reproduce the exact problem. The output here is documentation, tooling and pressure, not another account for you to make.

Not this

Not anti-technology

The objection is to a specific set of business incentives, not to progress, not to automation, and not to the people building things. Precision matters here.

Not this

Not a company

No funding round, no growth target, no exit. That limits how fast this can move, and it is the right trade for a project whose entire value is that it is not for sale.

Accountability

How you would know it worked.

A campaign with no failure condition is just a vibe. These are the things that should be measurably true in a year, and if they are not, that belongs in the log too.

A A guide that a non-technical person can follow start to finish without help
B A public, cited record of what has been lost, updated continuously
C People outside the founding group shipping changes without being asked
